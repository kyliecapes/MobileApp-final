## New App Ideas - Evaluate and Categorize
1. Self Care/Wellness Tracker
  - **Description**: Bloom is a personalized self-care tracker designed to help users stay consistent with habits that support physical, mental, and academic wellness. Users log daily activities like hydration, sleep, mindfulness, journaling, or workouts and see their growth through streaks, icons, and visual dashboards. Bloom aims to make self-care feel motivating and aesthetic, turning everyday habits into a rewarding “personal glow-up journey.” The app may also include an AI-powered reflection feature where users describe their recent habits and goals and receive personalized suggestions.
  - **Category**: Health & Wellness
  - **Mobile**: Mobile enhances the experience through push notifications for daily habits, quick logging from anywhere, and the camera to capture journaling photos or progress pictures. The mobile format also supports short daily check-ins and streak tracking, making it more than a website—it is a guided, on-the-go self-care companion.
  - **Story**: The story focuses on empowering users to maintain balanced wellness in their busy lives. It transforms routines into visual “growth,” making self-improvement feel satisfying and personal. Students and young adults would connect deeply with the idea because many struggle to stay consistent with self-care, and Bloom brings structure and motivation to that process.
  - **Market**: The wellness app market is huge and steadily expanding. Bloom provides meaningful value to a wide group students, working adults, and anyone building a healthier routine. It also appeals to a niche: people who enjoy aesthetic, habit-focused lifestyle apps.
  - **Habit**: Bloom is designed to be opened daily, sometimes multiple times. Users create content by logging activities, writing reflections, and tracking their streaks. Visual progress keeps the app engaging, and AI-generated suggestions encourage users to return to monitor and improve their routines.
  - **Scope**: V1 can include 3–5 daily habits, a minimal dashboard, mood logging, and simple visuals—very achievable within the course timeline. V2 could add AI habit recommendations, editable habits, and enhanced graphics. V3 could incorporate rewards, more customization, or long-term insights. Even a stripped-down prototype communicates the full vision effectively.

2. Budget Tracker
  - **Description**: A simple and intuitive financial app that helps users log expenses, track spending categories, and monitor their budget over time. Users can enter purchases, categorize them, and view summaries that reveal patterns in their financial habits.
  - **Category**: Finance
  - **Mobile**: Mobile is essential because the user can log expenses instantly, right when they make a purchase. Optional features include camera receipt scanning, push notifications when approaching budget limits, and haptics for interactions. The convenience of real-time entry makes it far more effective than a website.
  - **Story**: The app helps students and young adults take control of their spending without overwhelming financial tools. It reduces money-related stress by giving users clarity and simple organization. Since many peers live on tight budgets, the app would feel immediately valuable and relatable.
  - **Market**: The market is large anyone who spends money can use a budget tracker. It also serves a niche audience of college students, first-time budgeters, and users who want something cleaner and simpler than complex banking apps. The value proposition is strong for personal finance beginners.
  - **Habit**: Users interact with the app several times per week, often daily. They create entries, categorize spending, and build budgets. They also consume spending summaries and category totals. The app becomes part of the user’s routine as they track and improve their financial habits.
  - **Scope**: V1 could include basic expense logging, categories, and a running total—fully achievable. V2 might add weekly/monthly summaries, charts, and savings goals. V3 could include receipt scanning or CSV exporting. Even a minimal version still provides meaningful financial insight.

3. Campus Map Helper
  - **Description**: A navigation app that helps students and visitors quickly locate buildings, classrooms, parking areas, and key campus services. Users can view their live location, tap on building markers, and access important information like office hours or department descriptions.
  - **Category**: Navigation/Education
  - **Mobile**: This app relies heavily on mobile features like GPS, real-time location tracking, and native maps (MapKit). The real-time “you are here” function is something a website cannot replicate well. The experience is designed around moving physically around campus with the phone in hand.
  - **Story**: New students and campus visitors often get lost or struggle to find buildings during the first weeks of the semester. This app solves a common frustration and offers a smooth, user-friendly alternative to outdated campus maps. Friends and peers would appreciate having an easier way to navigate campus.
  - **Market**: The target market is your university community (students, faculty, guests), which is focused but well-defined. The concept is easily expandable to other campuses or institutions, giving it broader long-term potential. Its value is highest for new or incoming students.
  - **Habit**: While not a daily-use app, it is extremely helpful during peak times (first weeks, schedule changes, events). Users mainly consume the map but may create value by saving favorite locations or personal routes. It’s used in short but meaningful bursts.
  - **Scope**: V1 includes a static campus map with tappable markers and building descriptions—very achievable. V2 could add live GPS tracking. V3 might include walking routes, bus lines, or event overlays. The core idea is simple and focused enough to complete within a semester.

Final App Idea Chosen: Self Care/Wellness Tracker
